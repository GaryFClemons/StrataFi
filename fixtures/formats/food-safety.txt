Food Safety and Allergen Standard
=================================

Version 4. Effective June 1, 2026. Owner: Quality and Food Safety Manager.
Approved by the Leadership Team on May 11, 2026. Replaces version 3.

1 Scope
-------

This standard applies to every café and to the roastery's tasting room. It
covers how milk, food, and prepared drink ingredients are stored and held,
temperature checks, allergens, handwashing, and when a sick employee stays
home. Where the local health code is stricter, follow the health code.

2 Holding temperatures and times
--------------------------------

Cold items are held at 41°F (5°C) or below, and hot items at 135°F (57°C) or
above. Label every opened or prepared item with the date and time it was
opened or made, and throw it out when its time runs out.

| Item | Hold at | Discard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dairy milk and cream, opened | 41°F (5°C) or below | 4 days after opening, or the printed date if sooner |
| Oat, almond, and soy milk, opened | 41°F (5°C) or below | 7 days after opening, or the printed date if sooner |
| Milk left in a steaming pitcher | Not held | After each drink; never re-steam |
| Cold brew concentrate | 41°F (5°C) or below | 7 days after the steep ends |
| Brewed iced tea | 41°F (5°C) or below | 24 hours after brewing |
| House-made whipped cream | 41°F (5°C) or below | 48 hours after making |
| House-made syrups | 41°F (5°C) or below | 14 days after making |
| Cut fruit and yogurt cups | 41°F (5°C) or below | 48 hours after prep |
| Breakfast sandwiches in the warmer | 135°F (57°C) or above | 4 hours after reaching temperature |
| Pastries in the case | Room temperature | At close: donate or log as waste |

3 Heating sandwiches
--------------------

Heat breakfast sandwiches in the oven to an internal temperature of 165°F
(74°C), checked with a probe thermometer, before they go in the warmer. Record
one probe reading per batch on the temperature log. A sandwich that drops
below 135°F (57°C) in the warmer is thrown out, not reheated.

4 Fridge and freezer checks
---------------------------

Read every fridge and freezer thermometer at opening, at 2:00 p.m., and at
close, and record the readings on the temperature log. Freezers read 0°F
(-18°C) or below. If a fridge reads above 41°F (5°C), check it again after 30
minutes. If it's still above 41°F (5°C), move everything to another fridge,
call the Facilities help desk, and tell the Café Manager. Throw out any milk
or food that has been above 41°F (5°C) for more than 4 hours, or for an
unknown length of time.

5 Allergens
-----------

5.1 What we track

Halcyon tracks the nine major allergens: milk, eggs, fish, shellfish, tree
nuts, peanuts, wheat, soy, and sesame. Almond milk and the hazelnut and
pistachio syrups contain tree nuts. Oat milk is not certified gluten-free.
Each pastry's allergens are printed on its label and listed in the allergen
binder.

5.2 Making an allergy order

When a guest mentions an allergy, add the ALLERGY modifier in the register so
it prints on the cup label. The barista making the drink washes their hands,
takes a pitcher from the clean rack, purges and wipes the steam wand before
steaming, and uses the purple allergen-safe blender jar for blended drinks.
Tell the guest that our cafés handle all nine allergens and we can't promise
any item is free of them.

5.3 If a guest has a reaction

Call 911 if the guest has trouble breathing, swelling of the face or throat,
or feels faint. Don't give the guest any medication, though they may use their
own epinephrine injector. Tell the Café Manager, keep the cup and any
packaging, and file an incident report in the safety portal within 24 hours.

6 Handwashing and gloves
------------------------

Wash your hands for at least 20 seconds at the start of each shift, after
breaks, after handling raw eggs, cash, or trash, and before putting on gloves.
Wear gloves to handle ready-to-eat food that won't be cooked. Change gloves
every 4 hours, and whenever they tear or you change tasks.

7 Illness
---------

An employee with vomiting, diarrhea, or jaundice doesn't work until 48 hours
after the symptoms stop. An employee with a sore throat and a fever works only
away from food and drinks, such as on the register, until the fever is gone.
Tell the person in charge before the shift; paid sick time covers the missed
shifts.
